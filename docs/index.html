The theorem

In every well-formed position with only a white king, a white queen and a black king, White to move and no castling rights, White forces checkmate within ten moves against every defence.

Axioms: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound only. No sorry, no decide. The same holds in the full game with every automatic FIDE draw rule (kqk_game_whiteWins_wellFormed), and with Black to move the four possible cases are proved (kqk_black_to_move).

Perfect play

Under the FIDE rules with automatic draws (fivefold repetition, the 75-move rule, dead positions), chess is a finite game. Zermelo’s theorem then says it has a value:

What is not known. Which of the three outcomes holds. Chess has about 4.8 × 1044 legal positions; the belief that perfect play is a draw is an opinion, not a theorem, and this project does not claim otherwise. What it does prove is the determinacy above and, position by position, the value of endgames.

The ingredients

Rules kernel

Squares, positions with castling rights and en passant target, pseudo-legal and legal moves, check, checkmate and stalemate: plain computable Lean definitions, read against the FIDE text.

Sanity suite

Move counts 20, 400 and 8,902 from the start and 48, 2,039 from Kiwipete, plus castling, en passant, promotion, pins and double check, all by kernel evaluation. False claims are rejected.

Game and draw rules

Game states keep their history. Repetition compares legal-move sets (FIDE 9.2.3), so a dead en passant square is ignored; the 75-move rule yields to checkmate.

Determinacy

Abstract backward induction for finite games, then chess: the length bound comes from fivefold repetition over a finite set of position keys.

Certificate

A Boolean check of one table entry, proved sound once. Symbolic lemmas describe king and queen moves, so the kernel only evaluates three-piece positions.

The full game

A rank (twice the depth) strictly decreases along every certified line, so no position repeats and the clock stays low: White wins the actual game.

Dead positions

King against king, king and knight, king and bishop: invariants closed under legal moves. A lone knight or bishop only attacks squares of one colour, so a checked king always has a flight square; the bishop can never capture the lone king. The initial position is not dead.

King and rook

The KQK pipeline with a rook: 5-bit depths up to 16, the same symbolic lemmas, the same soundness proof, a second 64-chunk kernel check; Black to move and the full game as for KQK.
